# 2026-10-20: the frog falls in 3D

## What happened

Dominic on round HF1 (yesterday: the sprite turned onto its side, squashed flat, or kicking):
"those are all terrible. Just rotating or squishing the image". He's right: every option moved the
same flat picture of a standing frog, so none of them could show a frog lying down.

So round HF2 poses the dead frog in 3D and bakes that, like everything else we draw.
`scenes/art_pixel/fall_concepts.gd` puts the whole unit together in 3D, the way the placement check does
(`tests/placement/rig3d.gd`: body, legs at their hips, head, armour, gear on their sockets). It then
lays it down: the body pitched so its sloping back is level, then rolled, and each leg turned at its
hip. For two of the poses the legs become a new posed model, `leg.frog@limp` (stretched out behind).
What it held drops flat on the ground beside it: spear, shield, banner, and in A the helmet, upturned
by its head. The lot is baked in 8 facings with the unit baker's camera, light, bands and outline.
The sheet tool draws these with two new column kinds, `corpse` (8 facings, down) and
`corpse_fall:<k>` (the fall frame by frame), for a unit ref with `concept.corpse`
(`tests/sheets/frog_fall_hf2.json`).

- **A belly up**: on its back, the pale belly to the sky, folded legs up and splayed, helmet off.
- **B on its side**: lying on its flank, the legs stretched out long behind, the upper one over the lower.
- **C face-down sprawl**: flat on its belly, the hind legs stretched straight back and wide.

If one is picked, the game would play the last hop, then the 3D frames (25/50/75/100%) as a
flip-book, and leave the corpse sprite down until combat_fx fades it. That costs 4 more bakes x 8
facings for each hopper recipe, so the bake time on the loader needs measuring.

## Bugs on the way

- The first try rolled the frog about its long axis without levelling it first, so its upright,
  sloping body stayed tall: it stood on its head. Pitching the back level first fixed it.
- The "out" turn for a stretched-back leg spun it about its own length, so it didn't move at all.
  Legs got a spread (yaw) turn instead.
- The dropped banner landed under the body and was hidden; it lies clear behind it now.

- The placement check passes on every row, but it checks the standing unit; nothing yet checks a

- A frog riding a newt falls the four-legged way and isn't a hopper. Frog crew on an engine fall as
  hoppers, so they'd get the picked pose too.

## HF2b: the sprawl in its own shadow

Dominic: "sprawl is probably the most realistic, but shadow makes it look like it's floating". Two
causes. First, the sheet drew the standing unit's round shadow, which doesn't match a frog lying
stretched out. Second, the frog really was floating: the dead frog still had its standing body, so it
rested on its far hand, braced straight down, with the belly about 6 units up and the legs 7 up.

- Each corpse now bakes its own shadow: the posed body pressed flat onto the ground
  (its footprint as the camera sees it), one pixel wider, nudged away from the light.
- `body.frog@limp`: the arms flung forward and up in the body's frame, so they lie flat once
  it's pitched nose down. The body slumps lower and wider, about the same volume (a frog has no ribs
  to hold it up): lying down, the round upright body was as tall as the standing frog.
- The sprawl pitched 40 degrees nose down, the legs dropped 50 and spread 42 to their sides, the chin
  turned 22 down onto the ground (the helmet goes with the head). Measured, not guessed: the belly
  rests on the armour's lower edge 2 units up (the worker's on the ground), legs and chin at the ground.

## Built (D-165)

Dominic: "Yes, build the sprawl into the game as the frogs' fall." The pose is the frog's data now
(`parts.json` body.frog `corpse`), the module is game code (`src/view/art/corpse.gd`), and the 3D unit
assembly moved out of the tests with it (`src/view/art/rig3d.gd`, `geom.gd`: tests aren't exported).
art_kit bakes three frames (40, 70, 100%) per frog recipe, the last with its shadow, and UnitView gives
one short last hop and swaps to them. HF1's 2D variants and D-106's upside-down flip are gone.

The new placement rules caught the sprawl as HF2b left it: the body 4 units up on its legs (they'd
been dropped too far), and the worker's basket landing inside its flung-out arm. Tuned by measuring:
legs dropped 44 and the chin turned 12 down. The belly now rests 2 units up on the kerchief, the legs
and chin on the ground; the near hand's item lies out past the arm. A fixture puts the frog back on
its braced hand and the check must fail it.

In a match (the showcase battle, viking frogs vs romans) the dead lie face down among the living. A cold
bake of a frog race's 17 corpse recipes is 10.7 s of renders, about 2 s with six workers, then cached.
