# Market context (October 2026)

Why this game, why now. From the research behind the project. Figures are as reported; some come
from aggregators, so treat them as rough.

## Industry

- **Spending:** money is still growing but splitting into a few huge winners plus a large, mostly
  unprofitable long tail. The squeezed middle is mid-tier AAA and new live-service bets.
- **AAA costs keep rising:** GTA VI (Nov 19, 2026) is estimated at $1-1.5B. Take-Two's CEO says AI
  isn't offsetting costs. Typical AAA budgets now run past $200M.
- **Live service consolidates:** it still earns (78.1% of Take-Two's fiscal 2026 net revenue was
  recurring spending), but new entrants get cancelled fast. Players have battle-pass fatigue.
- **Indie/AA wins on quality and buzz:** Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, Silksong and Balatro.
  Indies made ~$4.4B on Steam in 2025, about a quarter of revenue, but the median game earned ~$249.
  About 20,000 releases a year make **discovery the bottleneck**. Demos, Next Fest, streamers
  and word of mouth matter more than marketing budgets.
- **Workforce and AI:** 28% of developers laid off in two years (GDC 2026). 36% use generative AI,
  but 52% think it harms the industry. **Implication:** present AI as a player creation tool, not as a
  replacement for handmade art, and disclose its use on Steam.

## Hardware

- RAM prices are up sharply because AI data centres are buying supply. The PS6 may slip to 2028-29,
  with price estimates near $1,000. Steam Deck OLED prices rose by up to 46%.
- Generations are longer, and gains come from lighting and AI-assisted rendering rather than brute force.
- **Implication:** a 2D game that runs on what people already own, including handhelds, is well placed.

## Engines

- GDC 2026: Unreal 42%, Unity 30%, Godot 11% (mostly newer indie studios) as the primary engine.
- Godot is the growth story: 39% of GMTK Game Jam entries (13% four years earlier), 7.1% of
  scanned 2025 Steam games and 8.6% of unreleased ones. MIT-licensed with no revenue share. Its weak
  spot is console publishing.
- **Implication:** Godot is the right choice for a small 2D team.

## Genres

- **Rising:**
  - Small-group co-op ("friendslop": 4 of Steam's top 10 sellers in 2025).
  - Roguelites (~$500M on Steam in H1 2026, but crowded).
  - "Gambling with extra steps" roguelikes.
  - Extraction shooters.
- **Steady:** survival-crafting, grand strategy, city builders, farming sims, job sims.
  Single-player action-adventure and RPGs are still the base.
- **Weak:** VR, 2D platformers, pure puzzle games, new hero shooters, big new live-service launches.
- **Remakes and known IP** are safe bets; "Remake" was one of the most anticipated tags.
- **Implication for us:**
  - A classic-style RTS taps nostalgia for the genre without being a remake.
  - The generator gives it clip-able, streamer-friendly moments ("I asked for X, got this").
  - Bounded generation gives it replayability.
  - The planned co-op/2v2 lobby and P2P join-by-key fit the social trend.

## Positioning

- **The pitch:** "A classic RTS, but your civ is whatever you can imagine, and the game prices it fairly."
- **The clip:** Frogs vs Rats, or centaur Romans vs winged Greeks.
- **Differentiators:**
  - Creation built into an RTS. The classic RTS games lasted decades on community content.
  - Fair pricing of silly units.
  - Near-zero server cost via P2P, which keeps a cheap game playable for years.
- **Risks:**
  - Scope: an RTS is a big engine project; Beyond All Reason took years.
  - The vanilla game must be fun on its own.
  - AI backlash.
  - Moderation and IP of shared content.
