Age of Anything

Describe a people. Then lead them to war.

A real-time strategy game in the classic mould: gather, build, advance through the ages and fight. Generate your army from a growing collection of cultures and peoples.

Follow development

In development for PC, Mac, Linux and Steam Deck.

Generate your army

Every race is put together from a kit of bodies, armour, weapons and mounts, dressed in one of a dozen cultures. Ask for viking frogs and you get frogs in spectacle helms with bearded axes, scouts riding newts, and a hero called Elder Bjorn Raven-Feeder.

The race gets its own name, its own unit lines through the ages and its own bonuses. The same words build the same race on every computer, so a friend can face exactly the army you made.

  • 290 parts: 71 weapons and tools, 55 helmets and hats, 22 shields, 43 war machines and beasts
  • 7 peoples, three kinds of bear among them, in 12 cultures, with 6 mounts to ride
  • 332 words it understands, and more with each update
  • One test run: 2,160 races generated, 2,159 different armies, 15,902 different units
  • A hero for every race: 2,000 different heroes in that same run

Automatically balanced

Every part a unit carries has a price. Armour, a mount, a bigger body: each one adds to what the unit costs and how long it takes to train, so a bear in bronze costs a great deal more than a frog with a stick. The game prices every unit it makes, the strange ones included.

Counters do the rest. Spears stop riders and giants, riders run down archers, slingers beat bowmen. However odd the matchup, both sides paid for what they brought, and both have an answer.

  • 46,661 automated checks run on every change
  • Every soldier line fights every other at equal cost, in two ages
  • Mass battles of mixed armies, each fought twice with the sides swapped
  • Counters win about four in five of their match-ups at equal cost
  • Whole bot-against-bot matches between races, from both starting positions

Build your city

A people builds in its own style. Rats put up tin-roofed sheds and water-tank houses from the scrapheap. Frogs raise thatched huts on stilts, joined by boardwalks. Beetles dig mounds. Greeks and Romans build in stone and red tile, whoever they happen to be.

Six building styles so far, each with its own town centre, barracks, walls, castle and wonder. Towns come alive as they grow: smoke from the chimneys, lit doorways, sheep in the pens, workers carrying their loads home.

Mount the walls

Raise walls and your soldiers can walk along them: archers on the walkway, towers at the corners, gatehouses that open for your own people and stay shut to everyone else.

Getting through someone else's takes rams, ladders and siege towers, with catapults and bolt throwers to soften them up, each crewed by its own people. The frogs bring a ram called the Snapper and a catapult carried on a snail.

Led by heroes

Every race has one hero, a named champion with gear nobody else wears. Name one in your prompt or let the race choose its own.

A hero leads from the front and lifts the soldiers around them. If they fall, the town can bring them back for a price.

Seven peoples, twelve cultures

Frogs, rats, spiders, beetles, bears, centaurs and humans, each of whom can take up any of twelve cultures: its armour, its weapons, its colours and its names. Viking frogs, Egyptian spiders and Roman bears are all fair game. New peoples and cultures arrive with updates; the bears came this month.

Play with friends

Host a match on your home network and your friends join from the lobby, no accounts or servers involved. Play one against one, or two against two with bots filling any empty seat. Matches can be saved and picked up later.

Local network play today; online play is next.

Every game something new

Roll the dice in the lobby for a race you've never seen, or type one you've always wanted. Pick a battlefield, from an old forest to a river delta crossed by fords and bridges, or a siege map where one side starts behind walls.

The bots come in three strengths, and they play the race they're given: they scout, build to its strengths, pick soldiers to counter yours and attack in waves.

And if the kit doesn't have what you want, add it. Mods are plain data: new weapons, armour, creatures, cultures, words, even a whole era. Their units are priced by the same rules as everything else, so they stay fair.

Steam Deck and controllers

Made to be played with a pad as well as a mouse. Tap A on anything you command and its orders open on a ring around it: what to build, whom to train, where to send them. A reticle selects, a circle gathers an army.

Pick up the mouse at any moment and the game follows; the pad takes over again the moment you touch it.

Made by one guy and his AI

Age of Anything is made by one guy and his AI, and made with love. First prototype in 24 hours went from ideas to gameplay, and hasn't stopped growing since.
