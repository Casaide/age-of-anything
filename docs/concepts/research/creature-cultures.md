# Creature cultures: frogs, beetles, spiders, rats, bears and beavers (research, 2026-10-11)

How a creature builds should come from its body and its life, the way human vernacular
architecture comes from climate and materials. Each culture below starts from **real
architecture and real animal structures** (with links: open the Commons galleries for pictures),
then gives a brief in the six layers from `buildings.md` (materials, construction logic, shape
grammar, site, meaning, growth by age), then the per-building forms, and, for frogs, beetles,
spiders and rats, several distinct town-centre directions concrete enough to model or draw.

Every building keeps the scale ladder in `buildings.md` section 3: same envelopes as the human
cultures, doors at 1.1x infantry height.

The rule for all of them: **no stickers.** If a motif (a lily pad, a horn, a web, a cheese
wheel) appears on a building, it must be doing a job there: holding something up, keeping water
out, storing food, catching prey, raising young, or marking rank.

## 1. Frogs and other amphibians (the `swamp` family, "reed" architecture)

### What frogs need, and what that means for a building
- **Water and damp:** skin must stay wet; eggs are laid in water. Buildings stand *in* or *over*
  water, have pools inside, shade, and wet floors. A frog town without water is wrong.
- **Body:** squat, wide, long-legged jumpers, eyes on top. Doors are wide and low; ladders are
  pointless (they jump), so levels connect by jumping stones, ramps and stepping pads.
- **Life:** eggs to tadpoles to frogs. The town centre, which makes workers, is naturally a
  **spawning pool or nursery**: the genre's insect faction does exactly this with its hatchery
  [G13].
- **Food:** insects. Farms are not fields but **insect traps and lures**: glowing lanterns over
  water at night, nets, sticky reeds. (A real technique: [light traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect_trap).)
  A second option is a flooded rice-style paddy.
- **Sound:** frogs call. Barracks drums and horns are the frog war-call: gourd resonators and reed
  horns.

### Culture brief
1. **Materials:** reed bundles (the main material), reed mats, mud and clay, lily pads (fresh as
   shingles and sunshades, dried as leather), shells of freshwater mussels and snails, fish bone,
   gourds, cattail fluff (insulation, torches), and later turtle shell and lacquer.
2. **Construction logic:** cut reed, tie it into thick bundles, plant both ends in the mud and
   **bend the bundle into an arch**; set arches in a row, tie purlins along them, lay reed mats
   over. Ends are closed with lattice screens. The whole hall stands on a built-up island of
   layered reed and mud, or on mangrove roots. Wet clay daub where shelter is needed; pads as
   overlapping shingles in the rain.
3. **Shape grammar:** (a) the bundled parabolic arch and the barrel hall it makes; (b) the lattice
   end screen; (c) the tufted bundle column (a pillar of reed ending in a cattail tuft); (d) the
   round reed-mat island with a sloped rim; (e) the pool. Every frog building is built from these.
4. **Site:** on water or wet ground, raised on reed islands or roots; boardwalks of reed bundles
   between buildings; a ring of duckweed water around the town.
5. **Meaning and motifs:** concentric ripples (painted on mats, cut into clay), egg clusters
   (dots in a jelly ring), the eye pair (two raised domes), the cattail tuft. Spawn means
   prosperity; ripples mean a voice carrying over water. **Team colour:** the dyed bindings that
   tie every reed bundle (a colour band on every arch, which reads as rhythm at 1x), plus painted
   spots on gourd drums and the door mats.
6. **Growth by age:**
   - Age 1: small reed-arch huts (3-4 arches), one lattice end, mats, a spawning pool ringed with
     stones.
   - Age 2: full mudhif halls (7-9 arches), tufted columns at the door, clay daub, mussel-shell
     inlays, pad-shingle roofs.
   - Age 3: twin or crossed halls (a vaulted cross), tall tufted towers, lacquered turtle-shell
     panels, lotus-seed lanterns, a raised clay dais around the spawn pool.

### Frog building set (from the brief)
- **House:** a 3-arch reed hut on a small round reed island, a lattice end with a low wide door,
  pad shingles on the weather side, a gourd water jar, a short jumping-stone path. Two or three
  houses can share one island.
- **Barracks ("call hall"):** a long 7-arch hall with both lattice ends open, a practice pool in
  front with floating reed targets, and a big gourd drum (team-colour spots) on a reed stand: the
  war-call.
- **Stable / large-unit building:** a **dock** for mounts: a reed jetty with a penned water area
  where giant newts or salamanders (or water-beetles) are tethered; a reed boat with a carved
  prow pulled up.
- **Watchtower:** a tall tufted reed-bundle pylon (two bundles leaning together like the mudhif
  door columns) with a woven lookout basket near the top and a reed horn. The cattail tuft is the
  crown element.
- **Farm:** a square of shallow water edged by a woven weir, with three or four glowing lure
  lanterns (lotus seed heads holding fireflies or glowworms) on bent reeds over it, and nets.
  (Ages: lanterns multiply; age 3 adds a clay-walled paddy.)

## 2. Bugs: beetles and spiders are two cultures

Today both get the `mound` architecture. They should split: **beetles dig, pile, roll and
farm; spiders hang, stretch, spin and ambush.** Beetles build in compression (earth stacked up);
spiders build in tension (silk pulled taut). That one difference gives two completely different
silhouettes.

#### Beetle culture brief
1. **Materials:** soil pellets and spit-cement, chewed wood paper, shed wing cases and chitin
   plates, thorns, seed husks, resin (amber windows, glue), fungus, cut leaves, dung balls (yes),
   later polished and lacquered chitin and beaten metal from human scraps.
2. **Construction logic:** carry pellets, stack them in rings, spit to cement: walls rise as
   tapering, ribbed, fluted towers with **ventilation chimneys** (a mound breathes). Thorns are
   pushed in as scaffolding and stay. Shed wing cases are overlapped as roof shells. Food is
   grown inside (fungus) or rolled in (balls).
3. **Shape grammar:** (a) the fluted pellet spire with chimneys; (b) the ribbed parabolic shell
   (Musgum); (c) the wing-case shingle roof; (d) the rolled ball; (e) the thorn bristle.
4. **Site:** rising straight out of bare, packed earth (a cleared "parade ground" of tamped soil,
   their creep), half dug in, entrances at the base and partway up.
5. **Meaning and motifs:** the sun disc (the rolled ball as the sun, from the scarab), the horn
   (rank; the bigger the horn, the higher), the six-fold pattern (legs, hexagons), the split
   wing-case line. **Team colour:** painted wing-case shingles and pigment bands on the spire's
   flutes (like the red patch on our beetle units' backs: keep that, it works).
6. **Growth by age:**
   - Age 1: low pellet mounds with a couple of chimneys, a rolled-ball granary, thorn fences.
   - Age 2: tall fluted spires with wing-case roofs and amber windows; the Musgum ribs.
   - Age 3: a "cathedral" cluster of buttressed spires, iridescent chitin crowns, a sun-disc
     finial, fungus terraces.

#### Beetle building set
- **House:** a ribbed parabolic shell (Musgum) 1.1-1.2 tiles tall, a round door at the base, a
  small chimney, the ribs painted at the tips in team colour.
- **Barracks:** a row of thin aligned pellet blades (magnetic-mound style) forming a walled
  drill yard; a sparring ring of thorns; horn trophies over the gate.
- **Stable / large-unit building:** a dug-out pen with an earth ramp, roofed with big overlapping
  wing cases on thorn posts; dung-ball "feed" piled outside.
- **Watchtower:** an organ-pipe tower of mud-dauber tubes, or one tall cathedral spire with a
  lookout hole near the top and a thorn crown.
- **Farm:** a fungus garden: a low crater of earth with cut-leaf compost and pale mushroom caps
  growing (age 3: a big mushroom fruiting body as the marker).

| [Bolas spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolas_spider), [Deinopis (net-casting spider)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deinopis) | Spiders that hunt with a silk ball on a line, or a net held between their legs | Unit weapons (see `units.md`); a net-rack outside the barracks |

**Games and films:** the weavers' silk dens of a bug metroidvania [G15] and its sequel's silk
kingdom [G42] (take: silk as architecture, with tension lines everywhere); the spiders of a bug
RPG [G16].

#### Spider culture brief
1. **Materials:** silk (sheets, cables, tubes, cocoon felt), stakes and thorns as masts, soil
   and moss (for lids and camouflage), collected bits stitched into cases (twigs, leaves, prey
   husks, beetle shells), dew drops (lenses, lamps).
2. **Construction logic:** set a few masts (thorns, twigs, stakes); run anchor cables between them
   and to the ground; spin sheets and funnels in tension between the cables; dig a retreat below.
   Everything hangs; nothing is stacked. Repairs add new layers, so old buildings get fuzzy and
   grey with old silk.
3. **Shape grammar:** (a) the saddle-shaped sheet between masts; (b) the funnel door; (c) the
   hanging cocoon or case; (d) the hinged round lid; (e) the radial wheel.
4. **Site:** anchored to the ground by guy-lines (a ring of anchor pegs around each building
   reads as "spider" at once); burrows below; the town connected by overhead cables.
5. **Meaning and motifs:** the radial wheel, the eight-point star (legs, eyes), dew pearls,
   wrapped trophies (prey cocoons hung like hunting trophies on the hall). **Team colour:** dyed
   silk: the radial threads of the wheel banners, bands on the cocoons, the funnel's lip.
6. **Growth by age:**
   - Age 1: single funnel sheets on twigs, lidded burrows.
   - Age 2: multi-mast sheet halls, cocoon houses, wheel gates.
   - Age 3: a great cable-net canopy over the town centre with hanging cases, captured beetle
     shells stitched in as armour plates, dew-lens lamps.

#### Spider building set
- **House:** a hanging cocoon or a caddis-style case on a tripod of thorns, with guy-lines pegged
  out; or a lidded burrow with a short silk turret.
- **Barracks:** a sheet-web canopy over a sunken sparring pit, net and bolas racks, a wheel gate.
- **Stable / large-unit building:** a tall tangle web between masts where the big spiders (or
  mounts) roost, wrapped prey hanging.
- **Watchtower:** a single tall mast with a purse-web tube up it and a lookout platform of
  stretched silk at the top; a dew-lens.
- **Farm:** a field of small sheet webs on low stakes catching flying insects (the lure is a dew
  shimmer); age 3: aphid pens (spiders don't farm aphids in life, but ants do: allowed as a
  culture choice, or swap for "fly nets").

#### Five spider town-centre directions

| | Direction | What you see | Inspired by | Why it's spider |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **The Funnel Lodge** | A big saddle-shaped silk sheet stretched between four thorn masts, sloping down into a dark funnel tube at the front (the door); anchor cables pegged out around it; wrapped trophies hanging under the sheet edge | [Funnel webs](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Funnel_webs), [Frei Otto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frei_Otto) | Pure tension architecture: no other culture looks like it. Risk: translucent sheets are hard to bake; needs a strong mast silhouette |
| S2 | **The Trapdoor Court** | A low earth platform with five round hinged lids (soil and moss on top, silk underneath), the central big lid propped open on a thorn, silk-lined turret rims; a tall wheel web on a frame behind as the vertical marker | [Trapdoor spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapdoor_spider) | The town lives underground and pops up. Lids opening is an animation for training. Risk: very low mass; the wheel frame must give height |
| S3 | **The Egg-Sac Tree** | A dead thorn tree wrapped in old silk, with cocoon pods hanging from its branches, the biggest (team-banded) at the centre as the mother sac; new workers drop from it on silk lines | [Spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider) egg sacs + [Social spider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_spider) | The spawn-point idea (like F2 for frogs) in spider form. Strong, creepy, funny silhouette; fits "spooky" prompts |
| S4 | **The Wheel Gate** | A cave mouth or earth bank faced by a huge orb web between two masts as the façade, the hall a silk-felt dome behind it; dyed radial threads in team colour; dew drops shining on the spiral | [Orb-weaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orb-weaver_spider), [Spider webs](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Spider_webs) | The most iconic spider image; the web reads as a big round emblem at any scale |
| S5 | **The Stitched Case Hall** | A tall cylindrical case of overlapping twigs, leaves, shell fragments and beetle wing cases, stitched with silk, hanging from a tripod of masts just above the ground; a silk ramp up to an opening | [Caddisfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caddisfly), [Bagworm moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagworm_moth) | Spiders as collectors: the building is a patchwork of trophies. Lots of texture, very vernacular |

**Recommendation:** S1 as the base grammar (sheets, masts, guy-lines), S3 or S4 as the town centre
(S3 for spooky prompts), S5's stitched cases for houses and the age-3 armour look.

## 3. Rats (the `sewer` family, "junk" architecture)

The rat set is the most promising today (tin-can house, plank-and-tin hall, sewer grate, cheese
wheel), but the town centre is a generic human plank house, and both are oversized (see
`buildings.md` section 3).

woodland war game [G25] (take: how each makes rodents martial without losing charm).

### Rat culture brief
1. **Materials:** human junk at rat scale: tin cans, bottle caps, crockery shards, bricks, pipe,
   rags, string, nails, matchboxes, cutlery, corks, buttons, cheese rinds, sacking.
2. **Construction logic:** scavenge, stack, lash with string, nail, weigh down with stones.
   Things keep their original shape (a can stays a can). Buildings **accrete**: new rooms are
   added on top of and against old ones, so age shows as height and clutter. Everything connects
   to the drains: doors come up from pipes and grates.
3. **Shape grammar:** (a) the can room (a cylinder with a cut door); (b) the stacked box tower
   (matchboxes, crates); (c) the patched corrugated lean-to; (d) the pipe (chimneys, tunnels,
   doors); (e) the grate.
4. **Site:** on brick and cobble with a drain grate in the yard; washing lines and string
   bunting between buildings.
5. **Meaning and motifs:** hoarding (piles of treasures by every door), the cheese wheel (wealth),
   the tail knot, the key (rats as clever thieves), crockery mosaic for status. **Team colour:**
   rag bunting on strings between buildings, painted bottle caps, rag door curtains.
6. **Growth by age:**
   - Age 1: one can or box with a lean-to, rag curtain, a grate.
   - Age 2: stacks of two or three, a pipe chimney, bottle-cap shingles, bottle-end windows.
   - Age 3: a cluttered tower block with crockery mosaic, a scrap spire, string lights.

### Rat building set
- **House:** a tin can on its side or upright with a door cut and its flap bent out, a bottle-cap
  roof patch, a stone on the lid, a rag curtain (resize per the scale ladder).
- **Barracks:** a crate fort: crates and a matchbox stacked into a wall around a yard, a
  sparring ring of corks, needles and pins in a rack.
- **Stable / large-unit building:** an upturned teacup or flowerpot shed with a pipe-mouth door
  for the riding beasts.
- **Watchtower:** a scrap spire: a drainpipe with a tin-can lookout on top and a spoon as a
  sunshade.
- **Farm:** a grain sack spilling into a walled patch, or a rubbish-heap garden (peelings and
  sprouting seeds).

| | Direction | What you see | Inspired by | Why it's rat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **The Grate Hall** | A brick sewer arch rising out of the ground with a grate as its gate; a shanty of tin and planks built on top of the arch; pipes as chimneys; a cheese wheel on a rack by the door | [Cloaca Maxima](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Cloaca_Maxima), [Storm drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_drain) | Rats come from below: the town centre is the way into the drains |

## 4. Bears and beavers (later; briefs only)

### Beavers
- **Real:** [Beaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver), [Beaver dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_dam),
  [lodges gallery](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Beaver_lodges),
  [dams gallery](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Beaver_dams); [Muskrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskrat)
  (smaller reed lodges).
- **Take:** the lodge (a dome of crossed sticks and mud with an underwater entrance), the dam
  (a curved wall of sticks holding back a pond), gnawed cone-tipped stumps, canals for floating
  logs.
- **Construction logic:** fell by gnawing, float logs, interlace sticks, plaster with mud. Every
  building has a pond side.
- **Grammar:** the stick dome; the dam wall; the pointed gnawed post (gnaw marks *are* the
  ornament); the log raft; the canal.
- **Town centre:** a big lodge on a dam with a mill-race. Tower: gnawed-post stockade tower.
  Farm: a bark-peeling yard or a willow coppice.
- **Team colour:** painted stripes on gnawed posts, flags on the dam.
- **Games:** a beaver city-builder [G18] (take: two beaver factions with different looks from one
  material: wood, dams and water levels as the whole identity); the beaver species of a fantasy
  city-builder [G19].

### Bears
- **Real:** dens in hillsides; heavy log cabins of whole trunks; salmon weirs
  ([Weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weir), [Fish trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_trap));
  honey ([Beehive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beehive), [Honeycomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeycomb));
  [Longhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhouse) for the timber hall scale.
- **Take:** heavy, low, massive: whole unsquared trunks, boulders, turf; claw-scored posts;
  honey skeps; salmon drying racks.
- **Grammar:** the hillside den with a log portico; the whole-trunk cabin; the boulder wall; the
  skep.
- **Town centre:** a den dug into a mound with a big log porch and a boulder hearth. Farm: a
  skep apiary or berry thicket.
- **Team colour:** painted claw marks; cloth hung from the porch beam.
- **Reference:** the badger mountain fortress of [Redwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redwall)
  (take: big animals in heavy stone and timber halls).
- Note: avoid borrowing sacred bear ceremonies of real peoples; keep bears' culture our own.
